# RVS — Objection Handling

**Version:** 1.0 · September 4, 2026
**Scope:** What to say in the room. Positioning is in `RVS_Positioning.md`.

Every answer below has been rewritten for the reduced scope. The old answers leaned on benchmarking, cross-dealer data, a technician app and a four-tier price list — **none of that exists**, and claiming it now is both dishonest and unnecessary. The honest answer is usually stronger, because the product is small enough to demo instead of describe.

---

## The four you will always hear

### "Our DMS already does that."

You will hear this reflexively even from dealers whose DMS handles intake badly. What they mean is: *I don't want to replace software, duplicate work, or retrain staff.*

> "It doesn't replace anything. Your DMS opens the repair order once the unit is here. This just collects better information before that — and hands your advisor a block of text they paste straight into the complaint field."

The product is *before* the DMS, and the paste block is the proof. Never say "DMS replacement." Never criticise their DMS; they chose it and trained their people on it.

If they ask about real integration: we don't do one, on purpose. A paste block delivers most of the value today without a partner certification that runs three to six months.

### "Our customers will just call anyway."

Partly true, especially for older owners. But the RV buyer demographic has been shifting younger, and the objection is really about whether the form is easy enough to be used.

> "Some will, and that's fine — this doesn't replace your phone. It catches the ones who'd rather send photos at 9pm. No account, no app, works on a phone, about sixty seconds."

The design answer: no login ever, mobile-first, and a QR code the dealer can put on the website, at the service counter and in email signatures.

### "We don't have time to implement new software."

Correct, and this is the strongest card in the deck.

> "There's nothing to implement. Give me an email address and put a link on your service page. That's the setup."

One setting has to change for a location to work: the recipient address. Everything else has a default. If setup takes more than ten minutes, that is a product bug, not a sales problem.

### "Why would I pay for this?"

The one most founders skip. The dealer is comparing you to answering the phone, which they perceive as free.

For the mobile tech, the number is concrete: a wasted trip is $100–200 in time and fuel, and one avoided trip a month covers the tool several times over.

For a dealership, the arithmetic is staff time. Forty intake calls a day at three minutes each is two hours of advisor time. You do not need to claim you eliminate it — a meaningful dent is enough.

Do not quote a price. Pricing is unresolved (Q6 in `../RVS_Plan.md`). The pilot is free.

## Competitive

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| *"Doesn't Kenect already do this?"* | "Kenect handles the conversation. We put a written work-up in your service inbox — VIN, photos, the customer's own words, and answers to the follow-up questions. Keep Kenect." |
| *"Doesn't ServiceNomad already do this?"* | "ServiceNomad runs a whole shop end to end. We do one thing that happens before the shop starts. If you want an operating system, theirs is a real product — this isn't competing for that." |
| *"Lightspeed's Service Scheduler already reduces RECT."* | "Scheduling helps after the unit arrives. This helps before it does — the tech knows the symptoms, has the photos, and has a category before the bay door opens." |
| *"IDS has free VIN decoding now."* | "So do we, same source. The VIN is the easy part. The value is the photos, the description and the follow-up answers attached to it." |
| *"How is this different from a $30 CRM tool?"* | "QuoteIQ and RV Service Suite run your customer list and your invoices. This does intake. Plenty of shops would use both." |

## About the product's limits

Answer these straight. The scope is deliberate and saying so builds more credibility than hedging.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| *"Can I text the customer back from it?"* | "No, deliberately. That's Kenect's job and they're better at it. We send the request to you and a status link to the customer." |
| *"Can I see reports on my service department?"* | "No. It's a form and an email. If you want dashboards, this isn't that — and honestly you said you didn't want another dashboard." |
| *"Does it schedule the appointment?"* | "No. No calendar, no bays." |
| *"Will it write the repair order?"* | "No. It gives your advisor a paste-ready block so they don't retype the complaint." |
| *"Do I have to log in every day?"* | "No, and that's the design goal. The write-up comes by email, and the status buttons are links in that email. There's a web app for settings and history; you shouldn't need it daily." |
| *"What if the AI gets the category wrong?"* | "The customer can override it, and the category is advisory. The part that matters is their own description, verbatim, plus the follow-up answers — those aren't AI-generated content, they're the customer's answers." |
| *"Who else is using it?"* | Honest, in months one to six: "Nobody yet — you'd be the first, which is why the pilot is free and why I'll change whatever doesn't fit." Do not invent references. |

## Never say

- Anything about benchmarking, industry data, cross-dealer insight or OEM analytics. **The capability does not exist.**
- Anything about a technician mobile app.
- Any specific price.
- Any customer count or case-study statistic you cannot name.
- "No one else is doing this."
